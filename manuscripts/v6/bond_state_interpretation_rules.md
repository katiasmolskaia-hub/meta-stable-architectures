# Bond State Interpretation Rules

## Why this file matters

If Volume VI is going to introduce bond-state dynamics, then the equations alone are not enough.

The architecture also needs a readable interpretation layer that connects:

- bond variables,
- qualitative bond states,
- and the practical meaning of those states for crisis and re-entry.

This file gives a minimal interpretation scheme for reading bond states from a small set of variables.

## Minimal variables

Let:

- `B_{ij}` denote `bond viability (жизнеспособность связи)`,
- `F_{ij}` denote `bond freedom (свобода связи)`,
- `S_{ij}` denote `bond stability (устойчивость связи)`,
- `D_{ij}` denote `distortion load (нагрузка искажения)`,
- `E_{ij}` denote `re-entry support (поддержка повторного входа)`.

These variables should not be read as fully independent metaphysical entities.

They are only a compact operational layer for distinguishing qualitatively different bond conditions.

## Reading principle

The goal is not to assign one rigid label to every bond at every moment.

Instead, the goal is to detect broad bond regimes:

- weak but living,
- empty,
- restored,
- stabilizing,
- crisis-supporting,
- distorted,
- or overly rigid.

In this sense, the labels below are interpretation rules rather than absolute ontological classes.

## 1. Latent weak bond

A bond may be read as a `latent weak bond (латентная слабая связь)` when:

- `B_{ij}` is low or low-to-moderate,
- `F_{ij}` is nonzero,
- `S_{ij}` is low or moderate,
- `D_{ij}` is low.

Interpretation:

- the bond is not currently central,
- but it has not vanished,
- and it may still be reactivated later.

Its main role is to preserve possible pathways for later coordination.

## 2. Sustained weak bond

A bond may be read as a `sustained weak bond (поддерживаемая слабая связь)` when:

- `B_{ij}` is stably moderate,
- `F_{ij}` is moderate,
- `S_{ij}` is moderate,
- `D_{ij}` is low.

Interpretation:

- the bond is not dominant,
- but it remains functionally alive,
- and it continues to support future gatherability.

Its main role is to remain usable without becoming maximally active.

## 3. Functionally empty bond

A bond may be read as a `functionally empty bond (функционально пустая связь)` when:

- `B_{ij}` is low,
- `E_{ij}` is low,
- `S_{ij}` is low,
- `D_{ij}` is moderate or high.

Interpretation:

- the bond may still exist structurally,
- but it no longer supports gatherability,
- and it no longer acts as a credible path of return.

Its main role is almost none.

## 4. Restored weak bond

A bond may be read as a `restored weak bond (восстановленная слабая связь)` when:

- `B_{ij}` is rising after a lower state,
- `D_{ij}` is falling,
- `F_{ij}` remains nonzero,
- `S_{ij}` begins to recover,
- `E_{ij}` becomes positive again.

Interpretation:

- the bond is returning to functional life,
- but has not yet become a major stabilizing support.

Its main role is to become available again for later field use.

## 5. Stabilizing bond

A bond may be read as a `stabilizing bond (стабилизирующая связь)` when:

- `B_{ij}` is high,
- `S_{ij}` is high,
- `F_{ij}` is moderate rather than near zero,
- `D_{ij}` is low,
- `E_{ij}` is high.

Interpretation:

- the bond remains alive,
- preserves continuity,
- and actively supports gatherability under stress.

This bond is not merely persistent.
It is useful at the right moment.

## 6. Crisis-support bond

A bond may be read as a `crisis-support bond (кризисно-поддерживающая связь)` when:

- `B_{ij}` is high,
- `S_{ij}` is high,
- `E_{ij}` is very high,
- the bond becomes more active specifically under elevated crisis pressure,
- and `D_{ij}` remains controlled.

Interpretation:

- the bond becomes part of the temporary support scaffold of the field,
- especially during a hard episode,
- without necessarily becoming the permanent dominant form of relation.

Its main role is crisis-time support of collective re-entry.

## 7. Distorted bond

A bond may be read as a `distorted bond (искаженная связь)` when:

- `D_{ij}` is high,
- `E_{ij}` is low or negative,
- `B_{ij}` may still be moderate or high,
- `S_{ij}` may even appear high at the surface.

Interpretation:

- the bond is not absent,
- and may not even be weak,
- but it carries mismatch, spread, or the wrong local pattern.

This state matters because a bond may remain active while still harming the field.

## 8. Rigid bond

A bond may be read as a `rigid bond (жесткая связь)` when:

- `S_{ij}` is very high,
- `F_{ij}` is very low,
- `B_{ij}` is moderate or high,
- `D_{ij}` is not necessarily high at first,
- but `E_{ij}` falls when the field needs reconfiguration.

Interpretation:

- the bond is too fixed to adapt,
- and therefore may preserve relation in form while losing it in function.

This state is useful for Volume VI because it clarifies that stability alone is not enough.

## Minimal summary rule

The central reading principle is simple:

- a good bond state is not the state with maximal stability,
- and not the state with maximal freedom,
- but the state in which `re-entry support (поддержка повторного входа)` remains positive and usable under changing field conditions.

In this sense, the operational target of Volume VI is not bond persistence alone.

It is the preservation of bond states that keep the field re-gatherable.
