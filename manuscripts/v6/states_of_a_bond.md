# States of a Bond

## Why this file matters

If Volume VI is going to treat the field as a living relational system, then the bond itself can no longer remain a passive edge.

The next architecture needs the bond to become a process element with its own state, transitions, and functional role.

## Core idea

A bond is not simply:

- present,
- absent,
- or weighted.

It may exist in qualitatively different modes.

These modes matter because a weak bond may:

- quietly preserve future gatherability,
- become functionally empty,
- become stabilizing under stress,
- or become a pathway of destructive spread.

## Minimal bond states

### 1. Latent weak bond

This is a weak but still living bond.

It is:

- low-energy,
- not dominant,
- periodically renewable,
- and still available for future reactivation.

Its main role:

- preserve possible pathways for later re-gathering.

### 2. Functionally empty bond

This bond remains structurally present but no longer carries living relational function.

It no longer:

- renews,
- supports gatherability,
- or reactivates meaningfully under stress.

Its main role:

- almost none;
- it is a relic rather than a route.

### 3. Stabilizing bond

This is a bond that becomes helpful under stress.

It does not dominate the field, but at the right moment it:

- reactivates,
- supports local coherence,
- and contributes to network-level return.

Its main role:

- act as a trusted low-cost route of stabilization.

### 4. Distorted bond

This bond does not merely fail to help.
It begins to pass the wrong thing.

Instead of carrying support, it carries:

- mismatch,
- instability,
- or spread of bad local structure.

Its main role:

- amplify or transmit destructive pattern.

### 5. Crisis-amplified bond

This is a bond that becomes temporarily stronger during crisis, but not necessarily in a healthy way.

It may:

- over-couple unstable agents,
- lock bad local synchrony,
- or intensify wrong collective motion.

Its main role:

- increase crisis coherence in the wrong direction.

### 6. Crisis-support bond

This is a temporarily strengthened bond that helps the field hold together under stress.

Unlike the stabilizing bond, which may remain weak and simply become relevant, the crisis-support bond is actively recruited into the recovery process.

Its main role:

- become a temporary scaffold of collective re-bonding.

## Main transitions

The next architecture will likely need transitions such as:

- latent weak -> functionally empty
- latent weak -> stabilizing
- latent weak -> distorted
- stabilizing -> crisis-support
- distorted -> crisis-amplified
- crisis-support -> latent weak

This means a bond is not static.
It moves through modes depending on:

- attunement,
- local compatibility,
- field gatherability,
- contagion pressure,
- and route of crisis entry.

## Working interpretation

A bond should be treated as a small relational organism inside the field.

Its importance is not defined only by strength.
It is defined by:

- whether it can reactivate,
- what it transmits,
- and whether it helps or harms the field's gatherability.

## Human translation

For humans, these states may resemble:

- a quiet but real tie that still holds trust,
- a formally existing but inwardly dead relation,
- a bond that becomes supportive in crisis,
- a relation that spreads fear or fragmentation,
- or a link that helps a scattered collective remember how to hold together.

## Why this is the next step

Once bond states are explicit, Volume VI can move from abstract discussion of field memory toward:

- measurable bond transitions,
- route-sensitive re-bonding,
- and experiments in collective re-entry after crisis.
