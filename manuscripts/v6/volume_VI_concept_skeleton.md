# Volume VI Concept Skeleton

## Working title

`Volume VI: Living Field Memory and Collective Re-Bonding`

## Central question

How can a heterogeneous field learn not only to receive coordination, but to preserve gatherability through crisis by remembering routes, reconfiguring weak links, and re-entering coherence appropriately?

## Main hypothesis

Recovery near harder regimes depends not only on a better instructor, but on the field's own capacity for:

- living memory of configurations,
- route-sensitive interpretation of crisis entry,
- collective re-bonding under stress,
- and stabilizing contagion of good local structure.

## New conceptual entities

### 1. Living field memory

Not memory of facts.

Instead:

- memory of field configurations,
- memory of relational tendencies,
- memory of what kind of structure helped the field stay gatherable.

### 2. Route-like memory

Not only where the field is, but how it got there.

This means:

- entry pathways matter,
- return pathways may differ even for similar visible crisis states,
- crisis diagnosis must include sequence, not only snapshot.

### 3. Collective re-bonding

The field reconfigures weak ties during stress.

This means:

- some links weaken,
- some strengthen,
- some temporary stabilizing substructures emerge,
- and the network may hold together through re-bonding rather than through top-down forcing alone.

### 4. Stabilizing contagion

Not only bad spread exists.

What may also spread:

- local coherence,
- correct pause behavior,
- support-ready link structure,
- and patterns that help re-entry.

## Diagnostic principle

The field should become the main diagnostic surface of the whole system.

That means:

- the field reveals the state of all participants together,
- the instructor reads that pattern,
- the agents participate in changing it,
- and the bonds themselves become part of the process state.

## Minimal first experiments

### Experiment A: Crisis bond experiment

Question:

- does temporary reconfiguration of links soften crisis more effectively than instructor refinement alone?

### Experiment B: Route-memory experiment

Question:

- can the system distinguish between two similar visible crisis states that were reached through different trajectories?

### Experiment C: Stabilizing contagion experiment

Question:

- can local recovery structure propagate through the field in a way that helps network-level re-entry?

## What should remain secondary at first

- full energy-cost formalization,
- quartz multi-frequency resonator ideas,
- rich ontology of collective memory,
- large philosophical framing beyond what the experiments can support.

These may become later layers, but should not overload the first Volume VI experiments.
